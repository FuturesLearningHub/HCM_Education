Individual cases go to PCB privately, never to a training-room discussion. Park the pattern, not the person.
If The room turns the accountability section into a grievance session about workload: Honor it once, then redirect: 'That's real, and it is exactly what the survey and community are for; make it a work item.' If A veteran HCM says their workarounds are faster than the new process: Don't argue speed; argue continuity and scale: 'Your workaround works because you're good. It strands the next HCM, and it disappears when you take a vacation.' Then recruit them: their workarounds are the best source of guardrail and job aid ideas in the room, and the champion network wants them.

06 · Tough questions, ready answers

Say each answer out loud once before you teach

Is this dashboard-and-escalation model just surveillance of HCMs?

The dashboards measure the work, not the person: overdue approvals, late transactions, training completion. Every one of those has a payroll error or a compliance exposure on the other end of it, and the same dashboards make GOOD work visible for the first time. The design goal is fewer 5 pm fire drills, and the escalation path only ever activates after a reminder was already ignored. Act on the first reminder and the whole apparatus stays invisible.

Why is training mandatory for HCMs when managers cause many of these errors too?

True, and the charter says so: many overpayments trace to HCM AND manager actions. Manager training is explicitly out of scope for this initiative, which is a scoping decision, not a verdict on who is at fault. HCMs are the through-point every transaction shares, so standardizing this role first prevents the most errors fastest. And an HCM who runs the playbook makes late managers visible instead of absorbing their delays quietly, which is how the manager side improves too.

I've done this job for years without a checklist. Why now?

Because the job got bigger while nobody was looking: more transactions, more systems, and now three new states with their own laws. The $84,000 in five months was produced by experienced people in busy weeks, not by beginners. The checklist is not a verdict on anyone's competence; it is what lets competence survive volume, vacations, and turnover. Pilots use them for the same reason.

What if Oracle's job aid is wrong or missing when I check it?

Then you have found real work, and there is a channel for it: Academy content sits under formal governance with defined ownership and a review cadence, and flagged content gets corrected for every HCM after you. In the moment, escalate the question to PCB rather than guessing. What ends is the era of silently working around a bad job aid; that silence is how they stayed bad.

Am I supposed to be the FLSA expert for my unit now?

The opposite. The bar is recognize and route: hear the overtime question, the custom-title request, or the out-of-state move inside an ordinary-sounding request, and flag it to PCB's specialists before processing. You are the tripwire, never the lawyer. The dangerous HCM is not the one who does not know an answer; it is the one who guesses one.

The manager won't approve timecards no matter how much I nudge. How is that my problem?

It is not your fault, and it is still your play: run the backup approval process before the deadline, and let the escalation and dashboard system do what it was built for, which is making the delay visible to someone with authority over that manager. What ends is the version where you personally absorb the chasing forever and the pattern stays invisible. The system is designed to move that weight off you.

Section 01 · The Role

The role nobody notices, until it slips.

HCM stands for Human Capital Management, and at Vanderbilt it is also a person: you, the specialist in each business unit who carries people actions into Oracle and works alongside PCB (People, Culture and Belonging) and the HRIS team. Until now the role has run without a standard: no checklist, no shared job description, training scattered across emails and webinars. The result is help desk volume, back and forth, and preventable pay errors. That is what the HCM Operational Excellence initiative is fixing, starting with this session.

A five-month window

Between January and May 2026, the initiative charter documents 24 overpayment cases in Engineering and the School of Medicine alone, totaling about $84,000. Most traced back to delayed or incorrect HCM and manager actions. Every one of them became a recovery conversation with a real person about money they may have already spent.

Go deeper

In practice

The initiative's core idea is moving quality upstream. Instead of relying on downstream reviews and help desk intervention, every recurring issue gets sorted into one of four fixes: training when people lack the knowledge, a system guardrail when Oracle can block an invalid entry before it saves, a guided workflow when instructions belong at the point of work, and an automated notification when a timely reminder would have prevented the delay. Today covers your half of that bargain: the knowledge and the habits. Oracle is being built to cover the rest.

Section 02 · The Core Four

The core four.

Four responsibility areas produce most of the preventable errors, and all four are won or lost on timing. Master these and you have mastered most of the role.

1 · Timecards

Approve on time

Every cycle, every deadline. An unapproved timecard delays or distorts someone's pay, and a rubber-stamped one hides overtime that must legally be paid. Review, then approve, before the cutoff.

2 · Transactions

Submit when you know

Hires, transfers, pay changes, assignment updates. The day you learn about a change is the day it goes into Oracle, with the true effective date, even if that date is later.

3 · Terminations

Process promptly

The classic overpayment is a termination entered after payroll already ran. Right date, entered the day you learn it, no waiting for it to feel official.

4 · Leave

Route it properly

Leave handled informally becomes wrong pay and compliance exposure. Get leave actions into the proper process early so pay, protections, and timecards line up from day one.

Play · Call the play

Why this matters: the core four are easy to recite and easy to fumble under real conditions. Five real-week scenarios; call the right move for each.

The one habit

Same day, into Oracle. If you learned about it today, it is in Oracle today, carrying its true effective date. A sticky note is not a system of record, and payroll cannot pay from your inbox.

Go deeper

In practice

Why timing beats memory: payroll runs on cutoffs, and anything that misses the cutoff either pays wrong or pays late. A change you hold until it "feels confirmed" usually crosses a cutoff while it waits. Overpayments are the worst version: the university has to recover money from an employee who may have already spent it, and underpayments hurt the person immediately. Same-day entry with the correct effective date lets Oracle and payroll handle the timing correctly; that is what effective dates are for.

Section 03 · Foundations

The concepts behind the clicks.

You do not need to be an employment lawyer to be a great HCM. You need to recognize when a routine-looking request touches one of three foundations, because that recognition is what tells you to slow down and verify before you click submit.

FLSA status

Exempt or non-exempt

The federal Fair Labor Standards Act sorts every job into exempt or non-exempt. Non-exempt employees track their hours and earn overtime when they work it; that is a legal right, so their timecards must reflect reality. Exempt status is a legal test about duties and salary, never a convenience setting.

Job architecture

The right box matters

Job families, levels, titles, and pay ranges form one standardized structure. Choosing the right job profile drives pay, FLSA status, and career paths. A custom title with a pay rate that "feels right" today becomes a classification and equity problem tomorrow.

Multi-state compliance

New states, new rules

Vanderbilt is expanding operations into New York, California, and Florida, and each state adds its own wage, hour, leave, and final-pay rules. Some states require a departing employee's final paycheck within days. When someone works from a new state, flag it early and verify.

Play · Spot the concept

Why this matters: these foundations never announce themselves; they arrive dressed as ordinary requests. Five requests; name the foundation each one touches.

Go deeper

In practice

The move is recognize and route, never diagnose alone. If a request touches FLSA status, classification, or a new work state, your job is to flag it to PCB's specialists before processing, with the details you already gathered. The authoritative primer on FLSA basics lives at the U.S. Department of Labor, and the HCM Academy's foundations content covers Vanderbilt's specific rules. What you never do is guess: a wrong guess here compounds quietly for months and surfaces as back pay, penalties, or an audit finding.

As a group · 4 min

Why this matters: the dangerous requests are the polite ones. Practicing the flag-it reply makes it easy to say in the moment.

  1. In pairs, one partner plays a manager asking: "Can we just make this role salaried so we can stop dealing with timecards?"
  2. The other practices the reply: yes to helping, and routing the classification question to PCB before anything changes.
  3. Swap, with a new request: "She's moving to California next month, nothing changes, right?" Practice flagging it early.

Flying solo? Draft your own one-line flag-it reply and keep it: helpful in tone, firm on routing, no diagnosis on the spot.

Respond: All three, and name the reframe: routing IS the helpful answer. 'Yes, and let me get classification to confirm so it sticks' serves the manager better than a fast yes that unravels in an audit. The flag-it reply you just practiced is a service, never an obstacle.

Debrief

Three triggers, one move: recognize and route. The dangerous HCM is never the one who does not know an answer; it is the one who guesses one.

Validate: 4+ of 5 on Spot the Concept; every learner has said a flag-it reply out loud once and it was helpful in tone, firm on routing.

Watch for: Learners trying to actually litigate FLSA rules in the room ('but what if the duties are…'). Redirect every time: the win is recognizing the question, not answering it. PCB's specialists answer it; that is the whole design.

Transition

“So where do the answers actually live now? One place, and that is new.”

60-min core: Core: cards in one line each, trainer stays at full length (it carries objective 3), pair practice becomes each person writing their flag-it reply in 60 seconds.

Section 04 · Oracle First

Oracle first.

The initiative establishes Oracle as the single source of truth for HCM training, procedures, job aids, and guidance. The scattered version of this knowledge (email threads, old webinars, that one person who knows) is being retired. What replaces it is built around how you actually work.

HCM Academy

Your learning path

Role-based learning paths built for what you actually process, organized by role type (staff, faculty, student) and transaction. Required courses are assigned, tracked, and certified, with completion visible to you and your manager.

Job aids & journeys

Help at the point of work

Step-by-step job aids and Oracle guided journeys sit inside the flow of the transaction, so the answer to "how do I do this" appears where and when you need it, kept current under formal governance.

Guardrails & alerts

The system helps you

Configurable guardrails validate entries and block invalid submissions before they save. Automated reminders surface what is due before it is overdue. The system is being built to catch what memory misses.

Play · Where do you go first?

Why this matters: every question routed correctly is a faster answer for you and a clearer help desk queue for everyone. Five moments; pick the first stop for each.

Quick check: Going forward, the single source of truth for HCM procedures, training, and job aids is…

Go deeper

In practice

The Oracle-first habit has a virtuous loop attached. Every job aid you use instead of opening a ticket keeps the help desk queue for genuine problems, which means faster help when you hit one. And when a job aid is missing or confusing, saying so through the feedback channel is real work: the Academy's content is governed, reviewed, and updated on a defined cadence, and your report is what triggers the fix for every HCM after you. Academy content also tells you why each requirement matters, so training connects to the errors it prevents.

As a group · 4 min

Why this matters: the initiative is actively deciding where to build guardrails, and the people in this room know exactly where they belong.

  1. In pairs: name one HCM task where Oracle should simply stop a bad entry before it saves. Be specific about what it should block.
  2. Share the best candidates with the room and collect them; real guardrail requests come from exactly this exercise.
  3. Notice the shift: from working around the system to shaping it.

Flying solo? Write down the one entry mistake you'd most like Oracle to make impossible, and submit it through the feedback channel this week.

Section 05 · Accountability & Community

Accountability, with backup.

The initiative pairs clearer expectations with real support. Expectations first: required training completed above 85 percent within its timeframes, transactions and approvals done by their deadlines, and overdue items that no longer disappear quietly. Reminders come first; what stays overdue escalates, and timeliness is visible on dashboards leadership actually looks at.

Quick check: You get an overdue reminder for a timecard approval and let it sit. In the new model, what happens next?

Now the support side, because accountability without backup is just pressure. The same initiative delivers the things HCMs have asked for:

A defined role

Checklist & job description

For the first time, a standardized HCM checklist and job description. Managers know what to hire for, you know exactly what the role expects, and "I didn't know that was mine" retires.

A community

HCMs, together

A real HCM community: a place to ask practice questions, share approaches, and raise ideas, with ongoing feedback channels into the Academy. HCMs asked for a forum; this is it.

A voice

Surveys & champions

The HCM surveys collect pain points and training needs, and a champion network of your peers supports the rollout. What you flag shapes what gets built next.

Quick check: You keep hitting the same confusing process, and half your unit hits it too. The move that actually fixes it is…

Go deeper

In practice

How success gets measured, so you know the game being played: adoption of the Academy as the primary source, required training completion above 85 percent within timeframes, a measurable reduction in preventable help desk tickets and payroll errors, and improved timeliness of timecards, approvals, and personnel actions. None of these is a gotcha metric; all of them fall out naturally when the room runs the core four on time and goes Oracle first. The dashboard is meant to make good work visible, and it will.

As a group · 4 min

Why this matters: the HCM survey is open input to the initiative, and a specific answer beats a vague one.

  1. Solo, two minutes: draft one line of survey feedback. Name the most confusing process in your unit and which fix it needs: training, a guardrail, a guided workflow, or a notification.
  2. Read a few aloud. The room votes: right fix, or would a different one land better?
  3. Keep your line; submitting it is part of the capstone's follow-through.

Flying solo? Draft the same one-liner and submit it through the survey or feedback channel this week. Specific beats vague.

Both halves are the same idea: the role finally gets taken seriously.”

Do
  1. State the expectations without flinching: training completion above 85 percent within timeframes, deadlines met, and overdue items that escalate instead of disappearing.
  2. Run the first quick check (the ignored reminder) and let its answer do the teaching.
  3. Frame before anyone bristles: reminders come first, escalation only ever follows an ignored reminder, and dashboards make good work visible too.
  4. Walk the support cards with genuine enthusiasm: the checklist and job description (first time the role has either), the community HCMs asked for, the surveys and champion network.

Expect:

  • Support, IF the dashboards also show the chronically late managers, not just HCMs
  • Surveillance worry from someone burned by metrics before
  • Pragmatists: 'fine, as long as the reminders are accurate'

Respond: Take the manager-visibility point and confirm it: the escalation path exists precisely so a manager's delay becomes their leadership's problem instead of your silent burden. For the surveillance worry: the metrics measure the work, and every metric has a payroll error on the other end. And yes, accuracy matters, which is what the feedback channel is for.

Debrief

Clear expectations, visible timeliness, and real support: a defined role, a community, and a voice into what gets built. Act on the first reminder and the whole apparatus stays boring, which is the goal.

Validate: Both quick checks correct; every learner has drafted one specific survey line naming a process and its fix type.

Watch for: The section tipping into grievance about workload or managers. One honoring sentence, then convert it: the survey line is where grievances become work items, and the escalation system is what stops HCMs from absorbing other people's delays silently.

Transition

“That's the whole system. Quiz first, then the card that turns today into a habit.”
